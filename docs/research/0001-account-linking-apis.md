# Research: free/near-free bank + credit card + investment account linking for a UK single user

Resolves [wayfinder ticket #5](https://github.com/jambo-aayan/aayan/issues/5), part of map [#2](https://github.com/jambo-aayan/aayan/issues/2).

**Constraint under test**: ADR-0001's "free to run" hard constraint, applied to real automatic bank/credit-card/investment account sync (not manual entry).

> **A note on sourcing.** This session's network egress policy blocks direct fetches to every vendor domain checked here (`gocardless.com`, `developer.gocardless.com`, `truelayer.com`, `plaid.com`, `yapily.com`, `moneyhub.com`, `enablebanking.com`, `actualbudget.org`, `openbankingtracker.com`, `web.archive.org` — all returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`). Every claim below is therefore sourced through web search rather than a direct page fetch; the **cited URL in each case is the official page the search results point to** (vendor pricing/docs pages, or the vendor's own GitHub/support-centre content), not a blog summary, and I've flagged the handful of claims that only surfaced through secondary write-ups. Before committing to a provider, re-verify pricing/limits by opening the cited URLs directly — they were not confirmed first-hand here.

## 1. GoCardless Bank Account Data API (formerly Nordigen)

**Status: closed to new signups, being wound down.** GoCardless stopped onboarding new Bank Account Data accounts from July 2025 onward. The official notice lives at [bankaccountdata.gocardless.com/new-signups-disabled](https://bankaccountdata.gocardless.com/new-signups-disabled), which blocks new integrations outright — existing accounts can keep running for now, but a brand-new project (this one) cannot sign up today. This is corroborated by the vendor's own [Bank Account Data status/error docs](https://developer.gocardless.com/bank-account-data/statuses/) and by a live GitHub issue tracking the same block ([adept/gocardless-to-csv#4](https://github.com/adept/gocardless-to-csv/issues/4)).

**What the free tier looked like while open** (for reference, since some existing integrations still describe it): the [GoCardless Bank Account Data quickstart guide](https://developer.gocardless.com/bank-account-data/quick-start-guide/) and third-party integration docs (e.g. [Actual Budget's GoCardless setup guide](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/gocardless/)) describe a free plan allowing **up to 50 bank connections ("end user agreements") per month** — far more than a single-user app would ever need — with no charge for read-only account data (balances, transactions). End user agreements defaulted to 90 days of transaction history and a 90-day continuous access window, configurable up to longer periods depending on the bank. Rate limits are enforced per access scope (details/balances/transactions each metered separately); GoCardless's docs and a [Firefly III community discussion](https://github.com/orgs/firefly-iii/discussions/9138) describe banks commonly being capped as low as **4 API calls per day per account per scope**, returned as HTTP 429 with `X-RateLimit-*` headers when exceeded — comfortably enough for a personal app polling once or twice a day, tight if you want near-real-time sync.

**Bottom line: this is not a viable choice for a new integration.** Even though it was (and technically still is, for grandfathered accounts) the best-fit free option on paper, new-signups-disabled makes it a dead end for this project as of today (2026-08-15). Do not build against it.

## 3. Investment account coverage

**UK Open Banking regulation (PSD2 / the Payment Services Regulations, enforced by the FCA) covers payment accounts, not investment/brokerage/ISA accounts.** Per the [FCA's own open banking and open finance page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/open-banking-open-finance) and its [research note on open banking/open finance in the UK](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-notes/open-banking-open-finance-uk), the current regulated scope is payment services; extending the regulated API standard to savings, ISAs, pensions, and investments is explicitly framed as a *future* "open finance" initiative, not something in force today. [TrueLayer's own regulatory explainer](https://truelayer.com/reports/open-banking-guide/open-banking-regulation-in-the-uk/) confirms the same scope. This means every mainstream Open Banking aggregator (GoCardless, TrueLayer, Plaid's UK product, Yapily) inherits that boundary: they can reach current accounts and credit cards at the big banks, but general-purpose investment platforms, ISAs, and brokerage accounts are outside the standard's mandated API.

The one partial exception found is **Moneyhub**, which markets itself as going beyond Open Banking into "Open Finance" — connecting to pensions, investments, mortgages, and property values in addition to bank accounts ([moneyhub.com/products](https://moneyhub.com/products/), [moneyhub.com/solutions/pension-and-wealth-solutions](https://moneyhub.com/solutions/pension-and-wealth-solutions/)). This coverage relies on a mix of regulated Open Banking, voluntary industry participation (e.g. pension dashboards standards), and likely some credential-based/screen-scraping connections for platforms with no open API — it is not a standard every investment platform participates in, coverage will be patchy per-provider, and Moneyhub has no public self-serve pricing, so it's not realistically reachable at this project's budget or scale regardless.

**Conclusion: investment account sync should stay manual entry for the MVP.** No free-to-run-compatible provider offers dependable investment-account coverage; even paid enterprise aggregators only cover it partially and by voluntary provider participation rather than regulation. This matches what CONTEXT.md already anticipates in the Account linking note.

## 4. UK bank coverage

- **TrueLayer**: broadest documented UK coverage among the aggregators checked — its [Supported Providers table](https://docs.truelayer.com/docs/supported-providers-table) and [UK banks & PSPs help-centre section](https://support.truelayer.com/hc/en-us/sections/5609027471633-UK-banks-and-PSPs) list 70+ institutions including HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest/RBS, Santander, Halifax, Metro Bank, Monzo, Nationwide, Starling, TSB, Tesco Bank, Virgin Money, Yorkshire Building Society, plus credit-card-focused providers like MBNA and M&S Bank. Effectively all major UK high-street banks and mainstream credit card issuers.
- **GoCardless Bank Account Data**: historically covered the same CMA9/major-UK-bank set (it used the same regulated Open Banking rails), per its [overview docs](https://developer.gocardless.com/bank-account-data/overview) — moot now given new-signups-disabled.
- **Yapily / Plaid / Enable Banking**: all three connect through the same UK Open Banking regulated rails (CMA9 + voluntary participants), so in principle they reach the same major banks; exact per-bank coverage should be checked against each vendor's own provider list before committing, since coverage of smaller/newer challenger banks and specific credit card issuers varies vendor to vendor and wasn't independently verified here.
- **Moneyhub**: covers banks via Open Banking plus its wider Open Finance connections, but is not price-realistic for this project (§2).

For this project's actual accounts (UK high-street current account + a mainstream UK credit card), coverage is very likely fine on any of the regulated-rail providers — the differentiator is pricing and signup access, not bank support.

## Recommendation

1. **Bank + credit card linking**: GoCardless Bank Account Data — the one provider that historically matched the "free to run" constraint outright — is now closed to new signups and cannot be used for a fresh integration. Of the remaining options, none of TrueLayer, Plaid, or Yapily offer a published self-serve free production tier reachable without a sales conversation; Plaid's free Trial tier is US/Canada-only and doesn't apply here. **Enable Banking's "Restricted Production" tier is the best next candidate** — it's specifically positioned for exactly this use case (a developer linking only their own accounts, no commercial volume) and is the option most consistently pointed to as the practical GoCardless replacement for indie/personal projects. Before building against it: verify its actual free/restricted-production terms, rate limits, and UK bank coverage directly at [enablebanking.com](https://enablebanking.com/pricing/) (this session could not reach that domain to confirm first-hand) — treat that as the concrete next step this ticket unblocks, rather than committing to it sight-unseen. If Enable Banking's terms don't hold up on direct inspection, the realistic fallback is that "free to run" and "automatic bank sync" are in tension for this project, and manual entry (already built) should stay the primary path for bank/credit-card data too, revisited only if a genuinely free option resurfaces.
2. **Investment accounts**: stay manual entry. No option checked — free or paid — gives dependable, regulation-backed API coverage of investment/brokerage/ISA accounts; even Moneyhub's broader "Open Finance" coverage is partial, provider-dependent, and priced for enterprise customers, not viable for this project's constraint. This isn't a "provider not chosen yet" gap the way bank linking is — it's a structural limit of UK Open Banking's current regulatory scope, so CONTEXT.md's existing framing (manual entry as the standing fallback for investments) should be treated as the answer here, not a placeholder.
